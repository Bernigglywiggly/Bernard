# Money Crimes 03 · The Salad Oil Swindle (1963): facts file (started 5 Oct 2026)

Secondary sources only so far; the film needs the primary record. Best primary: Norman C. Miller, *The Great Salad Oil
Swindle* (1965; Miller won a Pulitzer for his Wall Street Journal reporting on the case), the SEC and NYSE records on
Ira Haupt & Co., court records of De Angelis's 1965 plea and sentence, and Warren Buffett's partnership letters (for
the American Express investment).

## How the fraud worked (Wikipedia; TodayIFoundOut; MoneyWeek)
- Oil floats on water: tanks were filled mostly with water and topped with a layer of oil, so a dipstick sample from
  the top came up oil.
- Hidden compartments inside tanks; the same oil pumped between connected tanks so it was counted more than once.
- Later, forged warehouse receipts.
- American Express Field Warehousing kept Allied's own staff as custodians, men tied to De Angelis.
- Reported claims exceeded the US Department of Agriculture's figures for the whole country's stock (TodayIFoundOut:
  verify against Miller before using).

## Angle (CRAFT §1)
The inspectors checked exactly what they were shown: the top of the tank. Every safeguard was real and pointed at the
wrong place. Close on Buffett buying American Express in the slump: the scandal hurt the balance sheet, not the
business (labelled as his reasoning, from his letters).
