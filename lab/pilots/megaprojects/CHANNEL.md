## One-line promise

Every big build, with the real numbers, real photographs, and a straight answer on what is finished, late or disputed.

## Recurring structure (and how to keep it from becoming a template)

The five questions every episode answers, in whatever order suits the story:

1. **The problem**: what the place could not do before.
2. **The plan**: what was promised, by whom, for how much, by when.
3. **The hard part**: the one piece of engineering that everything hangs on, explained with a cutaway.
4. **The money**: the budget in the owner's own terms, who pays, and where sources disagree.
5. **Where it stands**: dated to the day of upload, plus **our own sum or verdict** (the authorship).

CRAFT s.1 says a fixed chapter order is what gets a channel flagged, so the order rotates:
cold open on the night it worked (this pilot) · open on the invoice (HS2) · open on a measurement (Kansai) ·
myth versus record (Messina) · follow one euro (Rail Baltica) · two countries, one tunnel, told from each end (Brenner).
Every film ends on a calculation or judgement the sources do not hand us, labelled as ours.

## Title patterns (6 words or fewer, 30-50 characters, one tension)

- `[Place]'s [Thing] Is [Years] Late`: Europe's Sunken Tunnel Is Years Late
- `Why [Project] Costs [Figure]`: Why HS2 Now Costs £100 Billion
- `[Country] Is [Verb]ing a [Thing]`: Denmark Is Sinking a Tunnel
- `The [Thing] That [Twist]`: The Airport That Keeps Sinking
- `[Figure] and Still Not Started`: €13.5 Billion and Still Not Started

## Thumbnail text patterns (3 words ideal, never repeats the title)

- A count: `4 OF 89` · `0 km BUILT`
- A delay: `2 YEARS LATE` · `2029 → ?`
- A single unit figure: `73,500 TONNES` · `13 METRES DOWN`
- A before/after price: `€714m → €2.3bn` (only when both ends are sourced)

Picture side: one real photograph or one cutaway, subject filling most of the frame, high contrast, no faces.

## The honest commercial note

One pilot proves the format, not the income. The niche examples (300k-500k views on channels under 1,000 subscribers)
were given in the brief and not re-measured here. Before building episodes 2-6, publish the pilot and read its first
48 hours: click-through on the title and thumbnail, and retention at 30 seconds. If both are weak, change packaging
before making more films.
