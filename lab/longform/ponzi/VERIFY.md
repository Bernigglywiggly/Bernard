# VERIFY · Money Crimes 02 · "The Original Ponzi Scheme" (Charles Ponzi)

Independent fact-check, 7 Oct 2026. Checked: `script.py` (narration), `film.py` (on-screen text), `POST.md`.
No existing file was edited. Not yet uploaded, so everything below can be fixed before it goes out.

## 1. Verdict

**PREMISE HOLDS** — the scheme, its terms, its collapse and its aftermath are well documented and the script follows them, but the cold open's image is back to front (the famous four-abreast line was people queuing to invest), the Supreme Court passage is out of order, and about a dozen details rest on Wikipedia alone.

How to read the statuses (strict):
- CONFIRMED = I opened a source that states it. Best sources opened: the Supreme Court's opinion in *Ponzi v. Fessenden* (1922), the National Archives' *Prologue* article on his inmate file, the Smithsonian's own Sidedoor transcript, American Heritage (Francis Russell, 1973), Boston.com (2015), BU Bridge (Zuckoff interview), Time (2017).
- UNVERIFIABLE = the only source I could open was Wikipedia (a pointer, not proof) or a search-result snippet. "Low risk" means I expect it to be right.
- Beat numbers = paragraph number inside the chapter, counting from 1.
- Zuckoff's book itself is not readable online; where I cite him it is through interviews and excerpts.

## 3. Must change

1. **Cold open (open 1-2) and the POST description.** The four-abreast line was investors.
   - Spoken: "Boston, July 1920. A crowd fills School Street, outside a small office. Working people, mostly. Some of them have put in their life savings. Two days ago they were queuing four abreast to hand their money over. Today they want it back. That morning, the city's biggest newspaper has started asking the question nobody wanted to ask: where is the money coming from?"
   - On screen: quote card “Where is the money coming from?” loses its quotation marks, or gains the sub-label "THE QUESTION BEHIND THE POST'S STORY"; it is not a headline.
   - Description: "A line of people four abreast stretches down School Street" becomes "A crowd packs School Street, wanting its money back".
2. **Assistant manager (montreal 2).** Spoken: "Ponzi rose to manager, and discovered how it was done." No on-screen text affected.
3. **"Invested again" (run 4).** Spoken: "It worked. People who saw the money being paid out so easily decided it must be safe. Many of them turned round, and left their money where it was."
4. **Supreme Court order of events (bill 3).** Spoken: "Ponzi pleaded guilty to a single count of mail fraud, and was sentenced to five years. But Massachusetts had charges of its own, and didn't want to wait. Could a state put a federal prisoner on trial? The question went all the way to the Supreme Court. Massachusetts won. It took three trials, but in 1925 he was given seven to nine years more." On screen: timeline unchanged (it is already right).
5. **"Federal prison" (bill 3).** Covered by the line above ("sentenced to five years"). He served it in the Plymouth county jail.
6. **Coffee and doughnuts (open 3, run 3).** Spoken: "He's handing out coffee and hot dogs." / "He walked the line, handing out coffee and hot dogs…" (the contemporary report). Or keep "coffee" alone.
7. **Barron and the coupons (post 3).** Spoken: "Then the numbers came out. To cover what Ponzi had taken in, about one hundred and sixty million postal reply coupons would have had to be in circulation. There were only about twenty-seven thousand." (Drops the attribution to Barron, which I could not support; keep his name for the next line, which is supported.) On screen: unchanged, but only if the two figures are found in Zuckoff or the Post; otherwise replace both counters with the Post's 30 July headline: "NOT ENOUGH IN WHOLE WORLD".
8. **"By May, $420,000" (promise 6).** Spoken: "By May, the money was coming in by the hundred thousand. By June, two and a half million…" On screen: drop the $420,000 counter or source it from Zuckoff.
9. **"Less than thirty cents" (bill 2).** Spoken: "Years later, his investors got back about thirty cents on the dollar." On screen: "UNDER 30¢" becomes "ABOUT 30¢".
10. **The cheque (montreal 4).** Keep $423.58 only after checking Zuckoff; a safe line is "he forged a cheque for a little over four hundred dollars". On screen: counter becomes "$400+ · A FORGED CHEQUE" unless confirmed.
11. **Morse and the soap (montreal 6).** Spoken: "Morse fooled the prison doctors, it was later said, by swallowing soap, to make himself look gravely ill."
12. **Brazil (end 1).** Spoken: "His last job was in Brazil, working for an Italian airline. The war closed it down, and he was left with nothing."
13. **Montreal year (montreal 1).** Spoken: "Around 1907, he moved to Montreal". On screen: "MONTREAL · c. 1907".
14. **Five foot two (king 1).** Spoken: "Ponzi was barely five foot two". On screen: fine as is, or "BARELY FIVE FOOT TWO".
15. **Mansion details (king 2).** Spoken: "He bought a mansion in Lexington, a chauffeured limousine, and gold-headed canes." Drop "custom-built" and the diamonds unless sourced.
16. **"Thirty years" (again 4).** Spoken: "He had first landed in America thirty-one years earlier, and never become a citizen."
17. **Florida hedge (again 1).** Optional: "Some of it was under water." The hedge can go; this is on the record.
18. **Archive credit.** "'DON'T BE PONZIED' · BANK ADVERTISEMENT, 1920".
19. **Sources line in POST.md.** Add American Heritage (Russell, 1973), *Ponzi v. Fessenden* (1922) and the National Archives' *Prologue* piece if these fixes are adopted; the "Smithsonian: about $15M from about 40,000" figure in the docstring could not be checked (page blocked).
