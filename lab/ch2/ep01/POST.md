## Description

In 2025, American Express paid Delta Air Lines $8.2 billion: more than Delta's entire profit before tax that year. It wasn't buying seats. It was buying miles.

An airline's loyalty scheme is a currency it prints. It sells miles to banks for cash today, the banks hand them to cardholders for everyday spending, the airline alone decides what a mile buys, and some are never spent at all. In 2020, when the planes were grounded, United borrowed against MileagePlus, a scheme valued at $21.9 billion, while the stock market valued the whole airline at about $10.5 billion. This is how that machine works, who pays for it, and what it means for anyone collecting miles.
