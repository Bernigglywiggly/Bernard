# Retention playbook: what keeps people watching, from our own channels first (written Tue 27 Oct 2026)

TEAM queue item from 25 Oct (file name kept as queued). Written by account C. **Read `ATTENTION_PLAYBOOK.md` first: this
file doesn't repeat it.** It adds (1) what our own channels have now shown, (2) what learning science says about long
explainers, (3) a 48-hour diagnosis table, and (4) three style recipes for ~10 s test shots (E's channel looks, or a
Curve long-form test), each built to these rules.

**Evidence grades** (same as ATTENTION_PLAYBOOK): **A** = meta-analysis or large controlled study. **B** = one solid
controlled or large field study. **C** = industry data with a stated sample. **D** = practitioner claim or untraceable
number. **P** = our own analytics (small samples: read the direction, not the decimals). **[K]** = background knowledge.
Method note: web search extracts only for the outside sources (this cloud session can't open pages).

## 1. What our own channels have proved (P)

| # | Rule | Our evidence | What it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The swipe decides distribution; retention after the swipe barely matters** | Short 1 (Roadster upside down): 50% stayed, 80.8% average viewed, 15.4k views. Shorts 2-3: 42-46% stayed but 84% viewed, 1.1-1.3k views: 10x less reach (CRAFT, 24 Oct) | All effort on frame 0 and the first second: the most physically impossible-looking frame plus a question about it |
| 2 | **The answer goes in line 1** | Steve Short: answer in line 1, 280 views in 2 days = 37x the channel median. The Escape Short fell from 73% to 47% watching at 0:07-0:10 during a definition line; its answer came at 0:44 (REVIEW_2026-10-14) | Never define before you surprise. Cut "It's the website where..." lines |
| 3 | **A new event beats a follow-up** | Same-event Roadster follow-ups: ~1.1-1.3k each. New events at ~9 h old (27 Oct 02:50): Microduck 3,027, Unitree 1,219, Tesla Q3 1,373 | The radar's freshness window (~48 h) is a retention rule too: the swipe is kinder to news people haven't seen explained |
| 4 | **One odd, concrete object holds people** | Money Crimes: death-certificate Short 63% viewed (50 s), money-printing machine 50% (78 s), best on any channel | Every Short is about a thing you can see (a certificate, a duck, a bike on water), not a topic |
| 5 | **Long-form at our size is a distribution problem, not a retention one** | Roadster film: 5 impressions, 0 views in 31 h (27 Oct). Earlier Curve films held 1.3-7%; the one long film that held (Money Crimes, Eiffel Tower) kept 34%, 5:01 average. 79% of Curve views came from search (Sep-Oct) | Long films only where search demand exists (radar §3), with the search phrase first in the title. No editing fixes zero impressions |
| 6 | **Viewers rarely subscribe from a Short** | 20 subscribers from ~23.5k Shorts views (~0.09%) | Every Short ends on the channel's promise visually; each Short names the film it belongs to only if the film exists |

## 2. Long explainers: what learning science adds (the ATTENTION_PLAYBOOK covers hooks and structure, not learning)

| # | Finding | Number | Grade | Rule for our long films |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Signalling works**: cues that point at what matters (arrows, highlights, on-screen keywords synced to the voice) | d = 0.38 across 29 studies, 2,726 people (ETR&D, 2020). Text-picture signalling r = 0.17, larger for viewers with little prior knowledge (Richter, Scheiter & Eitel, 2016) | A | Keep the evidence stamps, pointer moves and keyword overlays, timed to the spoken word. They earn their render time most for general audiences |
| 2 | **Segmenting works**: a long explanation split into clear chunks | Meta-analysis exists (Rey et al., 2019); effect size not retrieved this session | A (direction) | One mechanism per chapter; chapters named as questions; a visible "chapter turn" so a boundary doesn't read as an ending |
| 3 | **Shorter, informal and drawn-live beat polished lectures** | edX, 6.9M viewing sessions: shorter videos more engaging; informal talking-head beat high-production lectures; tablet drawing beat slides (Guo, Kim & Rubin, 2014) | B (large, observational) | Diagrams that build stroke by stroke as the voice speaks (DrawLine, Blender build-ups) beat finished slides that appear whole. A conversational narrator beats a newsreader |
| 4 | Redundant full captions over dense animation overload | Mayer's redundancy principle | [K] | Long films: keyword overlays + CC, not burned-in sentences (already ATTENTION_PLAYBOOK §1) |

## 4. Diagnose at 48 hours (for A, B and E after every upload; C writes the lesson into CRAFT)

| What Studio shows | Likely cause | Fix (one change only) |
|---|---|---|
| Short: stayed to watch under 55% | Frame 0 or line 1 failed | New frame 0 (the impossible-looking moment + ≤ 6 words) and the answer in line 1. Don't touch the middle |
| Short: stayed ≥ 70%, average viewed under 70% | A sag in the middle | Find the dip's second on the curve, cut the set-up or definition line there, add one visual change |
| Short: average viewed over 100% | The loop works | Keep the end-to-start match; copy it to the next Short |
| Short: good percentages, few views | Topic stale or follow-up | Check the event's age; move to a fresher clip (rule 3) |
| Long: CTR fine, under 50% at 0:30 | Thumbnail promise not on screen by 0:08 | Re-cut the opening to show the thumbnail's object first |
| Long: a cliff at a chapter change | The boundary sounds like an ending | Bridge with a forward tease ("the part nobody checked comes next") |
| Long: almost no impressions | Distribution, not retention (rule 5) | Shorts that point at it; search-led topics only; don't re-edit |

## 5. Three style recipes for ~10 s test shots (show him all three before any full production)

Each recipe is built to CRAFT §3 (no banned looks) and to the rules above, and has its own palette, type, sound and
narrator so it can never read as The Curve re-skinned (YouTube's inauthentic-content rule, CRAFT §1). All fonts are
already in the repo; all music comes from our own synthesised beds (nothing to claim).

### Recipe 1: "X-ray the clip" (The Curve: long-form chapters and Shorts)
- **Idea:** the real viral clip stays on screen; at the key moment it freezes and a camera-matched Blender layer peels open *inside* the footage to show the mechanism, then snaps back to real speed.
- **Look:** The Curve v2 palette (night `#14052E`, cyan `#3DFFF2`, magenta `#FF2BD6`, acid `#E6FF2E`), Archivo 900 + IBM Plex Mono, narrator Chris.
- **Why it holds:** the visual mystery stays the subject (rule 1), the reveal lands in the same frame people already find impossible (closure, ATTENTION_PLAYBOOK §3), and the build-up is drawn live (§2.3). Labels ride tracked points, as in Shorts 4 and 7.
- **10 s test shot** (Wed's motorcycle on water, `MOTO_WATER_FACTS.md`): 0-2 s real clip at speed, text WHY DOESN'T IT SINK? · 2-4 s freeze, the water turns translucent, pressure arrows grow under the boards · 4-7 s a speed dial drops, arrows shrink to a quarter at half speed (rows 73-74), the bike settles · 7-10 s snap back to full speed in real footage, one caption.

### Recipe 2: "Case file, one continuous take" (a money-crime or how-they-profit channel)
- **Idea:** one physical desk, shot macro, one unbroken camera move across real documents (filings, court records, receipts); every number is a stamp, a highlight or a pin landing on paper.
- **Look:** paper `#EFE8D8`, ink `#1A1A1A`, evidence red `#B3122E`, manila `#D8B26E`; DM Serif Display for headlines, Courier Prime for labels; low piano pulse; its own narrator (not Chris), first person ("I read the filing so you don't have to").
- **Why it holds:** one odd object per beat (rule 4), sharp sourced numbers read as researched (ATTENTION_PLAYBOOK §3), and a single continuous camera keeps it fluent with something new every 2-3 s.
- **10 s test shot:** 0-2 s macro on a real document line with the sharp number, stamp hit · 2-5 s the camera slides along a red thread to a second document · 5-8 s a highlighter sweep reveals the contradiction · 8-10 s push into one word, which becomes the title card.

### Recipe 3: "Scale ladder" (a what-if or how-it's-built channel)
- **Idea:** one object at true scale; the camera pulls back in one continuous Blender move past references everyone knows (a person, a bus, a stadium); the number counts up on the ground plane as each reference passes.
- **Look:** museum white `#F5F5F2`, graphite `#2B2D31`, safety orange `#FF6A13`, measurement blue `#2F6BFF`; Anton for numbers, Inter Tight for labels; bright marimba bed; its own narrator.
- **Why it holds:** each reference is a micro-payoff every ~2 s (pattern completion), the scale jump is the surprise, and one camera move means no cut to swipe on.
- **10 s test shot:** 0-2 s extreme close-up of the object, label "1 OF THESE" · 2-5 s pull back past a person and a bus, counter climbing · 5-8 s past a stadium, counter lands on the sourced number · 8-10 s hold on the final frame, which also works as the thumbnail.

**How to judge the three:** render each at 1080x1920 and 1920x1080, show him them side by side with one line each on cost
per film (Recipe 1 is cheapest: reuses the kit; Recipe 3 is heaviest: Blender). His pick is the gate (TEAM §3.6: first
video in any new style). After launch, log every film in the ATTENTION_PLAYBOOK §6 test ledger.
