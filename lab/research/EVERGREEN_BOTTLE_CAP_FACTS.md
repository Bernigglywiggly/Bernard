## Facts (V = two independent sources agree; P = primary)
| Row | Fact | Grade | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The crown cork (crown cap) was invented by William Painter of Baltimore and patented on 2 February 1892 | V | [National Inventors Hall of Fame: William Painter](https://invent.org/inductees/william-painter); [Wikipedia: Bottle cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottle_cap) (patent number: most sources say 468,258; one says 468,226: check Google Patents before showing a number, else show none) |
| 2 | Painter's original crown had 24 teeth (flutes) | V | [BYO: Crowning glory, a brief history of crown caps](https://byo.com/articles/crowing-glory-a-brief-history-of-crown-caps/); [Croxsons: how the crown cap revolutionised the drinks industry](https://croxsons.com/?p=1140) |
| 3 | Tests (reported as the 1930s) found 24 teeth made the cap grip too tightly to open easily; the industry settled on 21, the balance between a secure seal and easy opening, which became the standard | V | BYO (row 2); Croxsons (row 2); [Brewers Journal: crown caps and their role in your beer](https://brewersjournal.info/?p=19038) (re-read the exact wording before scripting) |
| 4 | Today's standard 26 mm pry-off crown has 21 corrugations (flutes) | P (supplier spec) | crown cork quality spec sheet "Tapa Corona 26 mm: Corrugations (Flutes) 21, PRY OFF" (supplier document hosted at beermex.odoo.com); count them on a real cap on build day |
| 5 | The teeth are the cap's skirt crimped around the bottle's lip; a liner inside the cap seals against the glass, and the teeth hold it there. An opener levers one tooth up and the crimp lets go | V | Wikipedia: Bottle cap; [University of Houston, Engines of Our Ingenuity, episode 3366](https://engines.egr.uh.edu/episode/3366) (re-read before scripting) |
| 6 | Verdict (ours): 21 isn't magic, it's a compromise: enough teeth to hold the pressure in, few enough that you can still get it off | ours | rows 2-5 |
