03 · Visual directions

Four ways to move the camera

Same graphite, same one turquoise, same line work. What changes is how the camera earns its moves. Each one is a small 3D engine written tonight, so any subject can go through it.

A · Exploded orbit

The accelerator comes apart

Lines form the parts (Tron-style tips), then the layers separate bottom-first while the camera orbits 108°. HUD callouts dock into a column, and one turquoise data path pierces every layer. After Daniel Simon's light cycle and Branch Education.

B · Type rail

Gliding over the words

Extruded wireframe letters lie on the ground like buildings and each word forms as the camera arrives. Then it rises to look down on WAIT. After GMUNK's TRON: Legacy titles and the Stranger Things main titles.

C · ASCII sculpture

A solid shaded in characters

A torus knot, lit and z-buffered into 14,400 characters a frame. It decodes in from noise and turns slowly. After donut.c, play.core and Acerola's ASCII shader.

D · Curve ride + vortex

Riding the exponential

A POV coaster along 1950→2026 that becomes a chase view as the curve goes vertical, then tips over into the ring tunnel. It pairs with the vortex sound: the hang, the endless fall, the landing.

04 · Sound palette

Mechanical, not magical

23 sounds, all synthesised, with no samples, so nothing can be claimed. 05 · Jungle beds

170 BPM, made from nothing

Home-made breaks (kick, crunchy snare and ghosts, hats, ride) chopped and rolled at phrase ends. A rolling sub glides between roots, with F-minor pads (Fm9 → Dbmaj7 → Bbm9 → C7sus4) and an FM Rhodes for the liquid one. No Amen recording, so there's no Content ID risk. The drums run at 170 while the pads and bass breathe at half time, so it flows under a voice.

The pilot's bed

Arranged to the voice: half-time under the open, the full break for the ride, drums out for the drop, an atmospheric break for 1848, full again for "right now".

Liquid

Rhodes, pads and a soft break. The most "flowy".

Rollers

A tight rolling break with a reese bass and sparse pads. Driving.

Dark

Heavier reese, darker pads, a drop in the middle. For the tech segments.

07 · Script

How the pilot is written

Techniques borrowed (structure, not content)
  1. Flat-premise cold open. "Hard being a chip in 2026." Stated as fact, no setup.
  2. "You" by line two. The viewer is inside the system.
  3. Micro-antithesis. "That's not a trend line. That's a launch ramp."
  4. Escalation ladder. "Decades. Years. Months."
  5. Borrowed-domain chiasmus. "They saw a gold rush. He saw a supply chain."
  6. Aphorism. "The shovel isn't the model."
  7. Deadpan callback. "From one chip to another."

From the santeluca breakdown: about 2 s per shot in his edits, cuts on every clause, sincerity applied to absurdity. We keep the principle but move the camera instead of hard-cutting, which keeps it calm. His shock humour stays out.
