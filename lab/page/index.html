03 · Episode two · the price war

EP02 · The Ninety-Minute War

On 22 September one lab cut its flagship's price by a fifth. About ninety minutes later its biggest rival launched two models at half the price. Why price is the only signal everyone can read, what a million tokens is in your units, the Red Queen, and a labelled what-if: when thinking is almost free, what gets expensive? Vertical, 2:47, same Six Floors format.

What's new since EP01
  1. The ruler is a price: dollars per million tokens on a log scale, cheaper to the right. Every cut lands as a tag, and 2021 → 2024 ($60 → $0.06) runs as a glowing trail.
  2. Glass price tags: the old price gets struck through, the new one lands, and the percentage stamps in.
  3. The Red Queen treadmill: a chessboard rolling towards you, two queens running on the spot.
  4. A quieter IMAGINE: the drums drop out for the big question, then "your time, your attention, someone who turns up".
  5. The mirrored close: −20% and −50%, and both labs still running.
Facts
  1. Both price cuts are from launch-day coverage; the 90-minute gap is single-source, and the video says so on screen.
  2. $60 → $0.06 is a16z's "LLMflation" analysis (Nov 2024) for the same level of capability.
  3. The Red Queen: Carroll (1871), borrowed by Van Valen (1973).

04 · Episode one · after the pollar inspo

EP01 · Sixteen Hours

Your inspo, taken apart and rebuilt one level up. Pollar's rigour (dates, sources, one metric running along the bottom, silence before each reveal, a mirrored close), plus three floors they never go down to: what it means for you, the idea underneath, and a labelled what-if. Vertical, 2:25, for TikTok and Shorts.

What pollar does (measured from the file)
  1. One arc: a present-state hook ("PM since 20 July"), a flashback with a number ("176 days earlier"), a chain of dated reversals, the hidden rule (ministers vote with the government, always), the ceiling (95 of 95), then a mirrored close.
  2. A spine you can see: a day-count timeline that gains a marker at every event.
  3. Silence as punctuation: 0.1–0.4 s hard drops right before seven reveals.
  4. A calm narrator: ~146 Hz, ~140 wpm, no adjectives, fragments for the big beats.
  5. A still, document-like screen: one red accent, highlighted quotes, waffle grids, sources at the end.
What EP01 adds
  1. A depth gauge (left edge): Ground → Mechanism → You → Idea → Imagine, then a climb back up to the Surface.
  2. A log-time ruler in place of their day count: the horizon marker slides from 2 seconds to 16 hours, collecting a dot for each year.
  3. The idea made physical: a powers-of-ten zoom as a sheet is folded 42 times, past a book, a person, Everest and the space station, to the Moon.
  4. IMAGINE: the vortex drop into an ASCII dream layer, dotted "if the trend held" markers, ghost cards, and two questions.
  5. Motion and sound: 228 task cards in 3D, the success curve, the 50% line, and mechanical cues, all with their silence cuts.

10 · Script

How the pilot is written

Techniques borrowed (structure, not content)
  1. Flat-premise cold open. "Hard being a chip in 2026." Stated as fact, no setup.
  2. "You" by line two. The viewer is inside the system.
  3. Micro-antithesis. "That's not a trend line. That's a launch ramp."
  4. Escalation ladder. "Decades. Years. Months."
  5. Borrowed-domain chiasmus. "They saw a gold rush. He saw a supply chain."
  6. Aphorism. "The shovel isn't the model."
  7. Deadpan callback. "From one chip to another."

From the santeluca breakdown: about 2 s per shot in his edits, cuts on every clause, sincerity applied to absurdity. We keep the principle but move the camera instead of hard-cutting, which keeps it calm. His shock humour stays out.
