# Economy channel: working brief (drafted 8 Oct 2026)

Macro and household-level explainers for US adults: prices, wages, housing, debt, consumer behaviour.
Not The Curve (AI news), not How They Profit (one company), not Money Crimes (historical fraud).
No companies as protagonists, no crime stories, no AI. The protagonist is always **a household and a number**.

## 2. One-line promise

**Every number sourced, both sides of the ledger shown, nothing invented.**

Longer form for the About page: "Why your money feels the way it does, explained with official figures you can
check. Sources and dates for every number are in each description."

## 3. Recurring structure (a spine, not a template)

CRAFT section 1 bans a fixed chapter list, so only three things recur. Chapter count, names and order change each film.

1. **Two true numbers that disagree**, inside the first 20 seconds (the stake is the viewer's own household).
2. **The ledger**: the body weighs the credit side and the debit side honestly. The good news always gets a fair hearing.
3. **A verdict in the first person** ("my reading is...") plus "the one number to watch". No advice, no forecast.

House devices that make it ours:
- One worked household sum per film, shown as arithmetic on screen ("our sum"), with the inputs sourced.
- Provisional, revised or secondary data is said out loud in the line.
- One object carries across cuts: a paper ledger line or a receipt.
- Shapes to rotate: ledger (pilot), race between two lines, follow one paycheck, two streets, myth vs data, a timeline of one year.

## 4. Title and thumbnail patterns

Titles (CRAFT: about 30-50 characters, one tension; the niche tolerates up to about 60):

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Why [good number] feels like [bad thing] | Why a Record $87,000 Income Feels Like a Pay Cut |
| [Number] is back. [Who is stuck] | The 7% Mortgage Is Back. Half of America Can't Move |
| [Big number] at [rate]: [name for it] | $1.26 Trillion at 21%: The Credit Card Squeeze |
| America [did X]. That's [surprising reading] | America Added 29,000 Jobs. That's Almost None |
| Who pays for [price move]? | Gas Is Up 39% in a Year. Who Actually Pays? |

Rules: the title claim must itself be a sourced line in VERIFY.md (CRAFT, 6 Oct lesson). No "collapse", "crash", "it's over" unless a named source uses the word.

Thumbnail text (3 words ideal, never repeats the title, one figure maximum):
- the opposing number: `48.1`, `7.40%`, `+29,000`
- a two-word verdict: `BOTH ARE TRUE`, `NOT IN CPI`
- a split: `EARNED +33%` / `RENT +33%`
Look: black background, one monochrome object, one accent colour for the figure. No faces, no arrows-and-shock.

## 6. Risks to settle before launch

- **Inauthentic-content rule (CRAFT 1):** the first-person verdict, the worked sum and visible sources are the authorship. Keep them in every film.
- **No advice:** the scripts describe; they never say what to do with money.
- **Politics:** 2026's oil shock and rate rise are politically charged. The scripts name events and agencies, not parties or people.
