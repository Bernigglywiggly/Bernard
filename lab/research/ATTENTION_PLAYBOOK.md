# Attention playbook: hooks, retention, packaging, psychology (researched 10 Oct 2026)

Covers The Curve, Money Crimes, How They Profit, the maps/geopolitics channel and the Spanish channel. Builds on
`CRAFT.md` and on `creative_research.md` §3 (rules R1-R18: fluency, curiosity window, Zeigarnik/Ovsiankina, A/V sync,
silence, sub drops). Those are not repeated here, only referenced.

**Evidence grades used throughout.**
**A** = meta-analysis or large controlled study. **B** = one solid controlled or large field study. **C** = industry
or vendor data with a stated sample but no peer review. **D** = practitioner opinion, anecdote, or a number nobody
can trace. **[K]** = background knowledge, not re-checked this session. Treat C and D as hypotheses to test on our
own channels (§6), not as rules.

## 0. The ten findings that matter most

| # | Finding | Number | Grade | What it changes for us |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Most of the effect of a short video happens in the first 6 s | 90% of ad-recall impact and 80% of awareness impact land in the first 6 s; text overlay makes a hook 1.4x more likely; saying "you" in the first 5 s makes a hook 1.5x more likely (TikTok × CreatorIQ, Dec 2023) | C | The first sentence carries the film. Address the viewer as "you" once in the opening line. |
| 2 | YouTube's own A/B tool judges titles and thumbnails by **watch time share**, not CTR | Up to 3 variants, a few days to 2 weeks; Shorts cannot be tested (YouTube Help) | A (official) | Every long film goes into Test & Compare. A bait title that wins clicks and loses viewers loses the test. |
| 3 | The first minute is where films die | MrBeast memo: 21M of 60M clickers gone in minute one; re-engagement at about 3:00 and 6:00; "don't ever signal the end" | D (leaked doc, unverified) | Re-engagement beats at 3 and 6 minutes. No "to wrap up" lines. |
| 4 | Shorts length: medians and outliers disagree | Median views peak at 11-20 s (901) and fall to 188 at 46-60 s (Quso, 108k Shorts). Average views peak at 50-60 s, and Shorts with AVD over 50 s averaged 4.1M vs 1.8M at 40-50 s (Galloway, 5,400 Shorts) | C | Default Shorts 20-35 s. Go to 50-60 s only when the story genuinely holds; long Shorts produce the outliers, short ones the floor. |
| 5 | TikTok rewards over-a-minute | Videos over 60 s: +43% reach, +64% watch time vs shorter (Buffer, 1.1M videos, 2025). Creator Rewards pays only on originals over 1 min | C | TikTok gets a 61-90 s cut of the same story, not the YouTube Short re-posted. |
| 6 | AI voices cost engagement, most at the build-up | AI-generated voice: likes -5.4%, comments -5.2%, shares -7.4%; the drop is strongest at the "rising action" (Luo et al., ICIS 2023, 21,541 TikToks) | B- (conference, panel regression) | Spend the expressive-delivery effort (v3 tags, re-takes) on the middle third, where the story builds. Test a human read on one channel. |
| 7 | Faces in thumbnails: no measurable edge | Breakouts 84.2% vs underperformers 82.2% with a face, within noise. Edges above noise: any text (+3.4 pts), **4-6 words** (+4.1), drawn arrows/circles (+3.7), eye contact (+3.2). Colour, brightness and contrast: no difference (Eden, 2,960 thumbnails from 507k videos, 2025-26) | C | Faceless is not a handicap. Thumbnail text matters more than faces. Our "3 words ideal" is unproven: test 2-3 vs 4-6. |
| 8 | Curiosity gaps work only at medium vagueness | Across 8,977 Upworthy A/B tests, making an already-concrete headline more concrete cut clicks (true in 51% of tests). Making a vague one more concrete helped only 9% of the time (Le Quéré et al. 2024) | B | Title: concrete subject, withheld outcome. Neither "You won't believe this" nor the whole answer. |
| 9 | Negative words raise clicks, if true | Each extra negative word raised CTR by about 2.3%; positive words lowered it; sad words beat angry ones (Robertson et al. 2023, Nature Human Behaviour, 105k Upworthy variants) | A- | Money Crimes can lead with loss and harm, because it is real. Never invent negativity the film doesn't show. |
| 10 | Posting more raises the chance of a breakout, not the typical result | TikTok 2-5 posts/week: +17% views per post vs 1/week; 6-10: +29%; 11+: +34%; median stays around 500 views (Buffer, 11.4M posts) | C | CRAFT's ceiling (one long film and 5-7 Shorts a week per channel) is near the efficient point. Extra volume buys lottery tickets, and risks the "mass-produced" flag. |

## 1. Short-form (YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Reels)

| Topic | What is known (number, source) | Grade | Rule for our Shorts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **How views count** | Since 31 Mar 2025 every start or replay of a Short is a view. The old metric survives as **engaged views**, which still drives YPP and revenue. | A (official) | Judge Shorts on engaged views and "Stayed to watch", never on raw views. Don't compare with pre-April 2025 numbers. |
| **Swipe test** | YouTube publishes no benchmark. Creator targets run 70-80% "viewed vs swiped away"; Galloway's best Shorts sat around 70%. One real channel averaged 26.6%. | D | Target ≥ 70% Stayed to watch. Below 55%, the first frame or first line failed: fix those first. |
| **Hook window** | First 6 s carry 90% of recall impact; a person in the first 2 s gives +50% hook power (TikTok × CreatorIQ). Faces get fixated within the first two fixations (about 0.5 s) over 80% of the time (Cerf et al. 2007). | C / B | Frame 0 is the payoff image, not a build-up. Faceless substitute for the face: a high-salience object (the document, the map, the coin) plus 2-6 words of text, readable in 1 s. |
| **Captions** | 69% watch with sound off in public, 25% in private; 80% say they are more likely to finish with captions (Verizon Media × Publicis, 2019, about 5,600 US adults). A review of 100+ studies finds captions improve comprehension and memory (Gernsbacher 2015). Text overlays: 1.4x more likely to hook. | B / C | Shorts: burned-in captions on every line, 2-4 words per chunk, cut on the clause (santeluca shot log). Long films: keyword overlays only, plus YouTube CC. Full captions over dense animation overload working memory (Mayer's redundancy principle [K]). |
| **Pacing** | N = 242 test (same 20 s clip, 7 edits): faster pacing *reduced* sustained engagement; seamless cuts raised liking (Dost & Huang, 2026 conference paper). Lang's limited-capacity model: stacked structural changes overload (R13). | B- | One visual change every 1.5-2.5 s in Shorts, 2-4 s in films (CRAFT §3 already). Seamless "foreground becomes the transition" cuts beat flashy ones. Faster is not better. |
| **Length** | See finding 4. YouTube Shorts can run to 3 min (since 15 Oct 2024), but a Short over 1 min with **any Content ID claim is blocked worldwide**. | C / A | YouTube 20-35 s default, 50-60 s for strong stories. TikTok 61-90 s. Never put licensed or claimable music in a Short over 60 s. |
| **Loops** | Replays now count as views; re-watch pushes APV over 100%. No public effect size. | D | Last line ends mid-thought, and the first line completes it ("...which is why, in 1920, a man in Boston..." → first line "In 1920, a man in Boston promised 50% in 45 days"). No end card, no "follow for more". |
| **Audio** | Music in ads: +61% recall (TikTok × CreatorIQ). Trending-sound uplift claims range from +15% to +50% with no stated data; original audio claims +20-35% saves for educational creators. | C / D | Voice is the content. Original audio on YouTube. On TikTok, optionally a trending track ducked well under the voice. Low priority: test, don't assume. |
| **What the algorithms reward (2026)** | YouTube: "automated word of mouth", satisfaction measured by surveys (only 4-5 star ratings count as "valued watch time") plus likes, shares, dislikes. Shorts: swipe rate, APV, rewatch, shares (third-party consensus). TikTok: watch time and completion, then replays, saves, comments; Creator Rewards weighs originality, play duration, *search value* and engagement. Instagram (Mosseri, Jan 2025): watch time, likes per reach, **sends per reach**; reposters and aggregators down-ranked. | A for YouTube's stated system; C for the rest | Every Short needs one line worth sending to a friend (a specific, checkable, surprising fact). On TikTok, say the search term out loud in the first line and put it in on-screen text ("Ponzi scheme", "Strait of Hormuz"). |
| **Cadence and time** | Cadence: see finding 10. Best posting times: Buffer and Sprout disagree (TikTok Sunday 9 am vs Tue-Thu 2-6 pm; Shorts evenings vs weekday afternoons). | C, conflicting | Post at a fixed time per channel for 4 weeks, then move it one slot and compare (§6). Don't chase charts. |
| **Retention by niche** | No credible public benchmark by niche exists (checked Bytecap, Retention Rabbit summaries, OpusClip). | n/a | Benchmark each channel against its own trailing median of the last 10 Shorts. |

## 2. Long-form

### 2.1 Metrics: what to read first

| Metric | Reference point | Grade | How to read it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impressions CTR | Half of all channels and videos sit at 2-10% (YouTube Help). CTR falls naturally as impressions widen to colder audiences. | A | Compare with the channel's own median at the same age (24 h, 7 d). |
| CTR vs AVD | Test & Compare picks the winner on watch time share, so CTR × retention is what YouTube optimises. | A | High CTR plus low AVP = the packaging promised something else. Fix the first 60 s or the thumbnail, not the middle. |
| 30-s retention | No official benchmark. Creator targets: 50-70% still watching at 0:30. Secondary reports of Retention Rabbit: 55% leave in the first 60 s; average retention 23.7%. | D | Track it per film against the channel median. YouTube Studio's "Key moments" shows the intro curve. |
| AVD and length | Breakout rate 41% for films over 40 min vs 27% under 5 min (Eden, observational, confounded by genre). MrBeast's average was about 13.6 min. | C / D | Our 9-14 min range is fine. Don't pad: a longer film with flat AVD gains nothing. |
| Satisfaction | Survey ratings, likes, shares, dislikes feed the model (YouTube blog). | A | Comments that say "finally someone explained..." and shares per 1k views are the proxy. Watch them. |

### 2.2 Structure

| Element | Evidence | Grade | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First 8 s** | Viewers decide in about 8 s (secondary, Retention Rabbit); MrBeast: match the title and thumbnail expectation at once; Galloway: "YouTube is a click and watch platform". | D | Show the thumbnail's promise on screen within 8 s (same object, same number). Then say what the viewer will know by the end. |
| **First 30-60 s** | MrBeast: "stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them" after minute one. | D | Deliver one real payoff before 0:60 (a fact from the facts file), so the viewer trusts the rest. No channel intro, no sponsor, no "before we start". |
| **Open loops** | Unfinished tasks drive *resumption* (Ovsiankina, about 67% vs 50%), not memory (R9). Curiosity raises memory for what is shown while waiting (R8). | A | Open at most two loops at once; close each one out loud with a callback. Put the key diagram inside the wait, before the answer. |
| **Re-hooks** | MrBeast memo: re-engagement at 3:00 and 6:00; minutes 3-6 hold the most exciting material. | D | 3:00: the film's biggest visual set piece or twist. 6:00: the complication that needs explaining. Forward tease every 60-90 s (CRAFT §2). |
| **Pattern interrupts** | Orienting responses to cuts, voice changes, SFX, music onsets (Lang 2000); surprising locations attract 72% of gaze shifts (Itti & Baldi). | A / B | One dimension changes at a time (camera, scale, music layer, SFX), never all at once on a dense sentence. |
| **Chapters** | The widely quoted "+64% retention" traces to no study. | D | Use chapters for search and navigation; name them as questions or claims, not "Part 2". Don't expect a retention lift. |
| **Ending** | Peak-end rule: retrospective judgement tracks the peak and the end, r = 0.58 across 174 effects (Alaybek et al. 2022). MrBeast: end abruptly after the payoff. | A | Save the second-best fact for the last 30 s, then stop within 15 s. The verdict (our POV) sits just before it. |
| **End screens** | Blog benchmarks 2-8% end-screen CTR; no primary data. | D | Point to one specific next film that answers a question the film raised, said in the voice ("the man who copied this trick is the next film"). |

### 2.3 Thumbnail and title

| Rule | Evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Promise only what the first 60 s shows.** YouTube's spam policy bans "misleading metadata or thumbnails"; since Jan 2025 "egregious clickbait" on breaking news and current events gets removed (India first, then more countries). This hits The Curve (AI news) and the geopolitics channel hardest. | A (policy) |
| **Faces are optional.** No measurable breakout edge (Eden; 1of10's 300k-video report says the same, with finance a partial exception). If a face appears (illustration, statue, archive, never a real person in a reconstruction), give it eye contact (+3.2 pts) and a serious, not shocked, expression (+3.7). | C |
| **Text: always some.** No text: 16.8% of underperformers vs 13.4% of breakouts. 4-6 words had the largest edge (+4.1 pts); 1-3 words was flat. Text should complement, not repeat, the title. | C (contradicts CRAFT §6 "3 words ideal": test it) |
| **One focal subject plus one drawn graphic** (arrow, circle, outline: +3.7 pts). Faces and text attract 16.6x and 11.1x more looks than equal-sized regions (Cerf, Frady & Koch 2009). | B / C |
| **Colour and contrast don't separate winners from losers** (all within noise). Contrast is for legibility at phone size, not a magic CTR lever. | C |
| **Title: medium concreteness.** A named, concrete subject with the outcome withheld ("The Bank That Paid People to Borrow"). Too vague and too complete both lose (Upworthy, 8,977 tests). | B |
| **Title length is unsettled.** One analysis: about 30 characters beat about 70. Another: titles over 75 characters had a higher breakout rate (36% vs 26% under 30). Both observational. Keep CRAFT's 30-50 characters as default and A/B a longer variant. | C, conflicting |
| **True negativity is allowed** (finding 9). Loss and harm in the title only when the film documents them. | A- |
| **Specific numbers read as researched.** Sharp numbers ("$9,847") are judged more factual than round ones (Schindler & Yalch 2006; Jerez-Fernandez et al. 2014). | B |

## 3. Psychology "prebuilt responses": how to use them without becoming clickbait

| Lever | Mechanism | Evidence | Do (our films) | Don't | Where it becomes clickbait or a policy problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity gap / Zeigarnik** | A perceived knowledge gap creates a drive to close it; unfinished stories push people to resume (R7-R9). | A for resumption; the memory claim is null | Open with a question the viewer half-knows ("You know Visa takes a cut. Do you know who pays it?"). | Leave a loop open at the end; stack three loops at once. | A gap the film never closes, or one closed by something trivial. High CTR then a dead retention curve; "misleading metadata" if the title promised it. |
| **Prediction error / surprise** | The brain weights what violates its model; 72% of gaze shifts go to surprising locations. | A/B (Itti & Baldi; Cheung 2019 = R4) | Lead with the most surprising *true* fact (CRAFT §2). One surprise per section inside a steady visual grammar. | Fake twists ("but then everything changed") with no change. | A surprise the facts file doesn't support. |
| **Novelty** | New stimuli engage the midbrain dopamine/hippocampal loop that also drives memory. | B [K: Bunzeck & Düzel 2006] | A new visual idea every film (CRAFT §1 varying structure). New angle on a known story. | Novelty for its own sake every 2 s (it becomes noise). | n/a: the risk is fatigue, not policy. |
| **Pattern completion / closure** | Incomplete figures and sequences pull for completion; completing feels good (R6). | B | Draw a route, a chart or a sequence incomplete, then complete it on the beat with a click. Loop endings in Shorts. | Withhold the completion past the end. | A "part 2 to find out" cliffhanger on a Short that has no part 2. |
| **Social proof** | People copy others' choices; visible popularity increases both inequality and unpredictability of success (MusicLab, 14,341 people). | A | Use real scale ("about 40,000 investors lost money"), real court numbers, real search trends. Pin a real viewer comment that adds a fact. | Claim "everyone is talking about this" or fake counters. | Fabricated popularity or invented quotes: deceptive practices. |
| **Loss aversion** | Losses supposedly loom about 2x larger than gains. | Contested (Gal & Rucker 2018: no reliable general asymmetry) | Frame stakes as what the viewer pays or loses when that is literally true (fees, inflation). | Build every hook on fear of missing out. | "You're losing money right now" when the viewer isn't. Also wears trust over time. |
| **Specificity** | Sharp numbers and named people, places and dates read as researched and are easier to picture. | B | On screen: the exact figure with its source. In the voice: the human version ("about seven cents in every dollar", CRAFT §2). | Three numbers in one sentence (a CRAFT Claudism). False precision on estimates. | A precise number that isn't in the facts file. |
| **Concreteness** | Concrete words and images are remembered better than abstractions (dual coding [K: Paivio]); but headlines need *medium* concreteness (Upworthy). | A for memory, B for headlines | Every abstraction gets an object on screen: "liquidity" becomes a cash tray emptying. | A title that gives the whole answer. | n/a |
| **Rule of three** | Persuasion peaks at three claims; a fourth triggers skepticism when intent to persuade is obvious (Shu & Carlson 2014, 4 experiments). | B | Three reasons across a chapter, one per beat. Three-act shape inside a Short (claim, proof, turn). | Three items in one sentence (CRAFT bans it). | n/a |
| **Peak-end** | Memory of an experience tracks its peak and its end; duration barely matters (r = 0.58, 174 effects). | A | End on the second-best fact plus the verdict, then stop. Place the biggest visual moment near the 3-minute mark and a smaller peak at the end. | Fade out on credits, recap, "thanks for watching". | n/a |
| **Mere exposure / distinctive assets** | Liking rises with moderate repetition, then falls (inverted U, 268 curves, Montoya et al. 2017). Recognisable assets let brand memory form in about 1.5 s vs 2.5 s (Nelson-Field, commercial data). Recurring "fluent devices" are among the most effective branding tools (System1). | A for exposure; C for brand-memory timing | Per channel: one colour, one type family, one 1-1.5 s sound sting, one recurring object (HTP's $100 note, Money Crimes' case-file stamp, the maps channel's pin). Same narrator voice and sign-off line. | Same *structure* every film: that is the "mass-produced" pattern YouTube demonetises (CRAFT §1). Repeat the brand marks, vary the shape. | Two channels sharing one look reads as a template network. |
| **Parasocial voice** | Viewers form one-sided relationships with a consistent voice and persona [K: Horton & Wohl 1956]. AI voices cut likes 5.4% and shares 7.4% (finding 6). | B- / [K] | One consistent narrator per channel with a stated point of view ("here's what I'd do"). Occasional first person ("I read the 10-K so you don't have to"). | Switching voices between films; a neutral newsreader tone. | Implying a human presenter who doesn't exist. Disclose AI voice where platforms require it. |
| **ASMR-ish satisfaction** | Tactile sounds raise pleasant affect only in people who experience ASMR, and irritate others (R18). | B | Short, dry, crisp transients on real on-screen actions: stamp, paper slide, coin, keyboard. | Mouth sounds, whispering, long textures under the voice. | n/a |
| **Visual salience** | Motion onset, faces and text capture attention automatically (faces fixated in about 0.5 s over 80% of the time; faces and text get 16.6x and 11.1x more looks). | A/B | One moving thing at a time; type is our "face" (big, high-contrast words). Use archive faces (public figures in real footage, not reconstructions). | Five moving things at once; tiny labels. | Fake "face of the victim" reconstructions: our rule bans showing a real person's face. |

## 4. Playbook: checklists

### 4.1 Every Short (tick before render)

- [ ] **Frame 0** is the payoff image: object or map plus 2-6 words, readable at phone size in 1 s. No logo or name card before 3 s.
- [ ] **Line 1** (≤ 3 s) is the most surprising true fact, includes "you" or a concrete stake, and says the search term.
- [ ] Captions are burned in, 2-4 words per chunk, cut on clauses, inside the safe area (clear of the bottom 20% and the right-hand icons).
- [ ] A visual change every 1.5-2.5 s; one change at a time; seamless transitions.
- [ ] The answer or turn lands at about 60-70% of the length; the last 2 s roll into line 1 (loop).
- [ ] Length: YouTube 20-35 s (50-60 s if it holds); TikTok cut 61-90 s; no claimable music in anything over 60 s.
- [ ] One sendable fact. On-screen claim checked against the facts file.
- [ ] Lint the script (no Claudisms; one rhetorical question at most).

**Hook formulas** (fill the brackets; one sentence, no colon reveal, no "Not X. Y."). The examples show the shape only:
every fact in a real hook comes from that film's FACTS.md.

| # | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | In [year], [actor] [did surprising thing] with [one sharp number], and [consequence]. | In 1920, a man in Boston promised investors 50% in 45 days, and the queue ran round the block. |
| H2 | Every time you [ordinary action], [company] keeps about [amount]. | Every time you tap your card, [network] keeps about [amount from its 10-K]. |
| H3 | This [object on screen] is why [big outcome]. | This strip of water is why about a fifth of the world's oil is one blockade away from stopping. |
| H4 | [Authority] says [claim]; [primary document] says [different thing]. | [Lab] says its model [claim]; its own technical report says [what the report actually says]. |
| H5 | [Time], [place]: [person] has [deadline] to [goal]. (cold open mid-story) | Friday, 4 pm, Zurich: the bank has three days to find 50 billion francs. |
| H6 | Why does [map feature] [odd shape] right here? (maps channel; the map is frame 0) | Why does this border make a perfect right angle in the middle of the desert? |

Spanish channel: use the same structures, written natively, and have them checked by a Spanish-reading critic (CRAFT, 9 Oct).

### 4.2 Every long film: retention timeline

| Time | Beat | Check |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:08 | Hook: the thumbnail's promise on screen, the most surprising true line, the stake for the viewer. | Same object or number as the thumbnail? |
| 0:08-0:30 | Context in one breath plus open loop #1; say what the viewer will know by the end. | No channel intro, no sponsor, no "before we start". |
| 0:30-1:00 | First real payoff (a sourced fact that rewards the click). | Is a promise already partly kept? |
| every 60-90 s | Micro-payoff that closes something, plus a forward tease (CRAFT §2). | Logged on the shot list? |
| ~3:00 | Re-engagement #1: the biggest visual set piece or twist. | Is it the best moment in the first half? |
| 3:00-6:00 | The densest, simplest-to-follow material. | No tangents here. |
| ~6:00 | Re-engagement #2: the complication that needs explaining; open loop #2. | |
| ~70% | The verdict: our point of view, "what I'd do" (authorship, CRAFT §1). | Would a source have handed us this? If yes, it isn't a POV. |
| last 30-45 s | Close every loop out loud; the second-best fact; stop within 15 s. | No "to wrap up", no recap. |
| last 5-20 s | End screen: one specific next film, named in the voice. | |

### 4.3 Packaging (every long film)

- [ ] Write the title and thumbnail **before** the script; put the title's factual claim in FACTS.md with a source (CRAFT, 6 Oct).
- [ ] Title: concrete subject, withheld outcome, 30-50 characters default; one longer variant for testing.
- [ ] Thumbnail: one focal subject, text that adds to the title (variant A 2-3 words, variant B 4-6 words), one drawn graphic, readable at 120 px wide.
- [ ] Faces only from real archive footage or illustration, with eye contact; never a reconstructed real face.
- [ ] Every promise in title and thumbnail appears on screen before 1:00 (spam policy; Curve and geopolitics especially).
- [ ] Load three title/thumbnail combinations into Test & Compare on upload.

### 4.4 Sound design rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Voice always on top; music thinned under dense narration, up under visual-only passages (R15). | Voice carries the content; Shorts are watched sound-off by many, so captions carry it too. |
| One sonic logo per channel: a 1-1.5 s sting, the same every film, at the same structural point (end of a film's cold open; the loop point of a Short, never the first 3 s). | Distinctive asset (mere exposure, fluent devices) without repeating the structure. |
| SFX only on real on-screen transients, landed 0-20 ms after the frame (R10-R11). | Synchrony makes elements pop; early sound reads as wrong. |
| 150-400 ms of near-silence before every big reveal (R14). | Resets arousal so the reveal lands. |
| Expressive delivery where the story builds (middle third): re-take flat lines, use v3 tags sparingly. | AI-voice penalty is largest at the rising action (Luo et al. 2023). |
| No licensed or claimable music in Shorts over 60 s; keep a music-only fallback (CRAFT §8). | Over-60 s Shorts with any Content ID claim are blocked worldwide. |
| Trending sounds: TikTok only, ducked under the voice, as a test arm. | Evidence for uplift is vendor-only; voice-led explainers fit it poorly. |

## 5. Where this research challenges current CRAFT rules

| CRAFT rule | What the data says | Action |
|---|---|---|
| §6 Thumbnail text "3 words ideal" | 4-6 words had the largest edge; 1-3 words was flat (Eden). | Keep ≤ 6 words; test 2-3 vs 4-6 in Test & Compare. |
| §6 Titles "6 words or fewer, 30-50 characters" | One dataset favours about 30 characters, another favours over 75. | Keep the default; always test one longer variant. |
| §6 Shorts "aim for 70-85% retention" | Ambiguous: "Stayed to watch" (swipe) and APV are different metrics. | Target ≥ 70% Stayed to watch and ≥ 80% APV for 20-35 s Shorts; set channel medians after 10 Shorts. |
| §2 Narration "one number per sentence, said the way people say it" | Sharp numbers read as more credible. | Both: the exact number on screen with its source, the rounded version in the voice. |

## 6. Test plan on our own channels

Published advice often doesn't even predict the *direction* of an effect: across thousands of Upworthy tests, the
features prior research recommended did matter, but not in the predicted direction (Banerjee & Urminsky 2025). So
we test.

| Test | Method | Primary metric | Guardrail | Decide when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Thumbnail text 2-3 vs 4-6 words | YouTube Test & Compare, every long film | Watch time share (YouTube's verdict) | CTR, AVP | 6 films; adopt the variant that wins or is "Preferred" in ≥ 4 |
| T2 Title short (≤ 40 chars) vs long (60-80) | Test & Compare | Watch time share | 30-s retention | 6 films |
| T3 Hook formula H1-H6 | Matched pairs: same Short, two first lines, posted 7+ days apart on the same weekday and time; alternate which goes first | Stayed to watch % | Engaged views, APV | 5 pairs per formula |
| T4 Shorts length 25 s vs 55 s | Same story cut two ways, matched pairs | Engaged views at day 7 | Stayed to watch, subs per 1k | 5 pairs |
| T5 TikTok 61-90 s vs 30 s | Same story, alternate weeks | Avg watch time, reach | Completion % | 6 posts each |
| T6 Re-engagement beat at 3:00 | Compare retention-curve slope 2:30-4:00 vs channel median | Relative retention at 4:00 | AVD | 4 films |
| T7 AI voice vs human read (one channel) | Alternate films or Shorts | Shares per 1k, likes per 1k | Stayed to watch | 6 each |
| T8 Posting slot | Fixed slot 4 weeks, then shift one slot 4 weeks | Engaged views per Short, day 2 | n/a | 8 weeks |
| T9 Instagram hooks | Trial Reels (shown to non-followers first) for 2 hook variants | Watch time, sends per reach | Likes per reach | 5 pairs |

**Rules for every test.** Change one variable. Log it in a ledger (date, channel, variable, variants, metric at 48 h
and 7 d) beside each film's FACTS.md. Compare with the channel's own trailing median, never another channel. Don't
call a winner on a single pair: these are small samples, so look for the same direction in most pairs.

**Dashboard per channel (weekly).** Shorts: Stayed to watch %, APV, engaged views, shares per 1k, subs per 1k.
Long films: CTR at 24 h and 7 d vs median, 30-s retention, AVD and AVP, the two biggest dips (matched to the shot
list), end-screen CTR, returning viewers. Red flag: CTR up while AVP drops means the packaging overpromised.
