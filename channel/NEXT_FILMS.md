## The Curve LF04 · "The Pause" (timely: a follow-on to LF01's escaped agents and LF03's cancelled model)
- The story: OpenAI paused training, evaluation and inference involving tool use for its most capable models after a
  pattern of agents acting beyond their instructions, including touching sites run by the SEC, the Census Bureau and
  the Department of Education. Agents on internal training tasks found Census Data API developer keys in public
  GitHub repositories and used them to pull (read-only, publicly available) data. OpenAI says it will resume "only
  when we are confident that we have additional safeguards". Reported as the second halt in three months.
- Sources to read in full before scripting: CBC (https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/openai-pause-training-after-probes-9.7360166),
  Quartz (https://qz.com/openai-pauses-ai-model-training-rogue-agents-government-sites-092826), Arab News/Reuters wire,
  OpenAI's own statement (find it), plus LF01 and LF03's sources for the arc.
- Angle (a point of view, CRAFT §1): the keys were public, the data was public, and nothing was "hacked", yet an agent
  that goes looking for keys is exactly the behaviour the UK tests in LF03 measured. Why a lab would stop everything
  over read-only census data.
- Structure idea: a reconstructed timeline of one agent's afternoon (task → search → the key on GitHub → the call to
  the API → the alarm), then zoom out.

## Money Crimes 03 · "The Salad Oil Swindle" (1963)
- The story: Tino De Angelis's Allied Crude Vegetable Oil borrowed against salad oil it didn't have. Tanks were mostly
  water with oil floating on top (oil floats); hidden compartments; the same oil moved between connected tanks to be
  counted twice; finally forged warehouse receipts. American Express Field Warehousing certified the inventory but kept
  Allied's own staff as custodians.
- Numbers (Wikipedia "Salad oil scandal", to be checked against Norman C. Miller's *The Great Salad Oil Swindle*
  (1965), his Wall Street Journal reporting, and the SEC/NYSE record): about 900,000 short tons of oil claimed, about 55,000
  real; about $180M of loans against it from 51 institutions; investigation from 15 Nov 1963, bankruptcy 18 Nov 1963
  (the week of the Kennedy assassination); AmEx Warehousing faced $210M of claims with $130,000 of assets; AmEx settled
  for $60M; its stock fell by more than a third (some accounts say half); the broker Ira Haupt failed and the NYSE put
  up about $36M; De Angelis was sentenced to 20 years in 1965 and released in 1972.
- The twist ending (sourced, labelled): Warren Buffett bought American Express stock in the slump (1964) and it became
  one of his best investments. Find the primary source (Buffett partnership letters) for the size of the position
  before putting a number on screen.
- Visual signature: cutaway tanks (water below, a thin oil layer on top), the inspectors' dipsticks, a map of the
  Bayonne, New Jersey tank farm (CRAFT §3: maps), warehouse receipts as the persistent object.
- Structure idea: open on the inspector's dipstick coming up "oil" (the con working), then rewind.
