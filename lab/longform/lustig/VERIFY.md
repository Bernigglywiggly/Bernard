# VERIFY · Money Crimes 01 · "The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower" (Victor Lustig)

Independent fact-check, 7 Oct 2026. Checked: `script.py` (narration), `film.py` (on-screen text), `shorts.py`, `POST.md`.
No existing file was edited.

## 1. Verdict

**PARTLY HOLDS** — the American half (counterfeiting, the Times Square locker, the bedsheet escape, 20 years, Alcatraz, death in Springfield as "Robert V. Miller") is backed by primary documents, but the Eiffel Tower sale itself, the film's title claim, rests on one 1961 book with admittedly fictionalised scenes and no contemporary record I could find, and the film tells it scene by scene as plain fact.

How to read the statuses (strict):
- CONFIRMED = I opened a source that states it. Primary documents used: the Missouri death certificate and the FBI wanted notice, both already in `src/arch/`.
- UNVERIFIABLE = the only source I could open was Wikipedia (a pointer, not proof) or a search-result snippet; the better source was blocked or paywalled. Where I think the claim is probably right I say "low risk".
- Beat numbers = paragraph number inside the chapter, counting from 1.

## 3. Must change

Corrected wording for a re-cut or a replacement narration take. On-screen fixes are separate lines.

1. **The Eiffel sale is never hedged (open 1-5, sale, back; POST description).**
   - Spoken, add once at the head of chapter 4: "What follows is the story as it has been told ever since, first by the federal agent who finally caught him. No French record of the sale has ever turned up. But this is how it is said to have gone."
   - On screen: label "AS THE STORY IS TOLD" over the first Crillon shot; keep the existing AI RECONSTRUCTION tag.
   - Description: replace "This is the true story" with "This is the story as it has come down to us: the American half is on the record; the Paris half rests on later accounts."
2. **Cold open asserts the Capone con (open 5).**
   - Spoken: "Before he was finished, he would sell the Eiffel Tower, flood America with fake money, and climb out of a federal jail on a rope made of bedsheets. They say he even conned Al Capone."
   - Title: "The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower (And, They Say, Conned Al Capone)" or drop the bracket.
   - Capone Short: title "The Legend of the Man Who Conned Al Capone"; caption to begin "The story goes:".
3. **"Six" dealers (open 1, sale 2, back 4).** Spoken: "a handful of the biggest scrap-metal dealers" / "picked a new set of dealers". No on-screen number exists; nothing to change there.
4. **The 70,000 francs (sale 13).** Spoken: "He paid. By the usual telling, about seventy thousand francs changed hands." On screen: counter sub-label "FRANCS · BY THE USUAL TELLING".
5. **The Texas sheriff (box 6).** Spoken: "One story has a sheriff chasing Lustig all the way to Chicago…". On screen: "TEXAS → CHICAGO" becomes "ONE STORY HAS IT".
6. **Springfield, 1922 (box 7, map pin).** Spoken: "In 1922, under the name Robert Duval, he took a bank in Springfield, Missouri, for ten thousand dollars, switching envelopes of bonds." On screen: "1922 · 'ROBERT DUVAL' · $10,000" (drop "MAY").
7. **Watts and Shaw (money 2).** Spoken: "He teamed up with William Watts, a Nebraska druggist turned forger, who made the plates. By some accounts a chemist, Tom Shaw, worked with them." On screen: "William Watts — druggist, forger" / "Tom Shaw — chemist (by some accounts)".
8. **"Thousands of dollars … every month" (money 3).** Not wrong, but weak. Spoken: "For five years, fake notes poured into the American economy; by some estimates, more than a hundred thousand dollars a month."
9. **Billy May (money 5, locker 1-2).** Spoken: "The agents who caught him always believed the tip came from a woman: Mae Scheible, a Pittsburgh madam and his lover, jealous over another woman. Someone made an anonymous call, and told them where to find him." On screen: "BILLY MAY · HIS MISTRESS" becomes "MAE SCHEIBLE · THE SUSPECTED INFORMANT".
10. **Arrest date (locker 3).** Spoken: "In May 1935, agents arrested Victor Lustig in New York." On screen: "NEW YORK · MAY 1935 · ARRESTED".
11. **"The day before that trial" (locker 5).** Spoken: "On the first of September, 1935, with his trial still ahead of him, Victor Lustig escaped." On-screen date stays.
12. **Sentence date (escape 4).** Spoken: "In December 1935, he was sentenced to fifteen years…". On screen: "DECEMBER 1935" (or keep 9 December only after opening the NYT report of 10 Dec 1935).
13. **Death (rock 2).** Spoken: "Late in 1946 he was moved to the federal prison hospital in Springfield, Missouri, with what turned out to be a brain abscess. In March 1947 pneumonia set in. A day and a half later, he was dead. He was fifty-seven." On screen: unchanged.
14. **Radio label (tower 3).** On screen: "A RADIO MAST · FROM 1903" becomes "A RADIO MAST" unless the year is sourced.
15. **Escape Short headline.** "He escaped jail dressed as a window cleaner" becomes "He escaped jail pretending to clean the windows" (matches its own title).
16. **"The police caught up with him" (escape 3).** Spoken: "Then federal agents caught up with him in Pittsburgh."
17. **"Even his death certificate was a disguise" (rock 4).** Spoken: "Even his death certificate carried an alias."

## 6. Already public: how serious

Ranked by how much a knowledgeable viewer would object.

1. **The Eiffel sale told as documented fact, and "the true story" in the description.** High. This is the title claim. A viewer who knows the literature will say there is no record of it.
2. **Capone asserted in the title, cold open, pinned comment and the Capone Short's title/caption.** High for the Short (it travels without the film's chapter-level hedge in its packaging), medium for the film, where chapter 6 is honest.
3. **Billy May's phone call as fact.** Medium. It is the hinge of chapter 8 and it is an agent's guess.
4. **"The day before that trial."** Medium. Easy to disprove (Labor Day).
5. **Arrest on 10 May.** Medium-low. Contemporary press said 12 May.
6. **Death: "caught pneumonia … two days later".** Low-medium. The film shows the certificate that says otherwise (brain abscess, 36 hours).
7. **Watts/Shaw roles, "six" dealers, the 70,000 francs, the Texas sheriff, "MAY 1922".** Low. Variants within the legend.
8. **"A RADIO MAST · FROM 1903", "dressed as a window cleaner".** Low.

**Pinned correction or replace?** Nothing here is a fabrication by the film; every item is a known account repeated without its caveat. That is correction territory, not takedown. A pinned comment plus a description edit is enough for the long film, provided it says plainly: (a) the Paris sale rests on later accounts, with no French record; (b) the Capone story is a legend; (c) the tip-off was only suspected to be Mae Scheible; (d) the arrest was in May 1935 (10th or 12th by source), and he died of pneumonia following a brain abscess. Also change the description's "true story" line and retitle away from a flat "Conned Al Capone".

The one place I would replace rather than annotate is the **Capone Short**: its title and caption state a legend as fact, and a pinned comment on a Short is rarely seen. Re-export it with a hedged headline and caption, or change the title and caption in place if the platform allows. Replacing the long film is only worth it if a re-cut is being made anyway; if so, items 1 to 6 are each a one-line narration change.
