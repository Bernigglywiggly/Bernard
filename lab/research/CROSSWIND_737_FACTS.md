## 2. The physics (for the diagram)

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | **Crab:** fly the approach pointed partly into the wind, wings level, so the track stays on the centreline | F1 (via extracts) | V |
| 11 | The crab must come out "the instant before touchdown" by rudder, or "severe side loads" hit the landing gear (FAA wording, quoted second-hand) | F1 | V (copy the exact words from F1) |
| 12 | **Sideslip / wing-low:** upwind wing down, opposite rudder keeps the nose on the centreline; airliner pilots often combine the two (de-crab in the flare) | F1 | V |
| 13 | **Weathervaning:** an aeroplane has more side area behind the main wheels than ahead of them, so the main wheels act as a pivot and it tends to turn into the wind | F1 | V |
| 14 | It gets harder as the roll slows: the headwind part shrinks, the crosswind part matters more, and the ailerons lose effect | F1 | V |
| 15 | The fix on the ground: rudder or nosewheel steering for direction, aileron held toward the wind so the upwind wing doesn't lift | F1 | V |
| 16 | 737-800 crosswind figure used by most airlines: about 33 kt dry, 30 kt wet (demonstrated, not a Boeing-mandated maximum; varies with winglets) | F3 | U (secondary) |

## 3. The same runway before (context, not a cause)

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 22 May 2020: a BAe ATP (SE-MAO) left runway 33 at Birmingham after a go-around and a second approach; about 450 m off the paved surface; no damage, no injuries | F2 | V (AAIB) |
| 21 | AAIB's finding: the crew hadn't applied or held into-wind aileron in the landing and roll, and full rudder couldn't keep it on the runway; wind 230° at 14 kt gusting 27 kt, within the aircraft's limit but beyond the crew's experience | F2 | V |
| 22 | **Correction to radar 26 Oct:** the AAIB report doesn't use the word "weathervane" | F2 | V |
