- **Spoken hooks are concrete and personal, never the headline:**
  "Right now, your phone is drawing about one watt to play this." / "This is the light. Actual size, 2mm wide. It
  sits on a pair of Meta glasses… Maybe of you." / "An AI was given an exam in breaking into computers. It didn't
  just sit the exam. It went looking for the answer key."
- **Shape:** hook (an object, a number, a tiny story) → a scale ladder from the familiar to the huge (1 W phone → 400 W
  home → 1 GW reactor → 7.5 GW Paris → 10 GW campus) → the news, dated and sourced ("This week, the Wall Street Journal
  reported…") → the hidden mechanism ("the light blinks only for saving, not for the camera being on") → what is NOT
  known, said plainly ("Nothing is signed. These are talks, reported by one newspaper.") → a quiet, mirrored close →
  sources on screen.
- **Voice:** calm, declarative, no adjectives, no hype, short fragments for the big beats. 110-150 wpm.
- **Sound:** a low continuous bed; hard 0.1-0.4 s silences right before each reveal.
- **Look:** black, one accent colour, small monospace chapter labels ("THE LIGHT", "THE RULE", "WHO DECIDES"),
  big numbers set in full ("$250 000 000 000"), captions lower-middle. One visual system per film (a grid where area
  equals watts and the camera only pulls back; a network map with a live action log; grey 3D products on a ruler).
  Realism only where an object matters.

## Our rules (The Curve)
1. **Topic:** an AI / tech / power story with a number that can be made physical and a mechanism most people have
   never seen. Current (dated this week or month) beats evergreen; an evergreen story gets a current hook.
2. **Line one is concrete:** an object, a number or a tiny story, often in the present tense, often "you".
3. **Every number made physical:** a ladder from the viewer's hand to the scale of the story.
4. **Sourced and honest:** dates, named sources; say what's reported vs confirmed, and what nobody knows.
5. **Curiosity is the engine:** each line opens a question the next one closes. One clearly labelled IMAGINE
   (not a forecast, a what-if) stretches the viewer's imagination; it is never passed off as fact.
6. **No jokes, no swearing, no hype words.** Short declarative sentences; fragments for the big beats.
7. **Fast:** George at ~1.15-1.2 speed (≈170-190 wpm), tight gaps, but a hard silence before each reveal.
8. **Close quietly:** a mirrored pair of lines, then sources.
9. **Look:** ASCII all the way (characters on black, turquoise accent, monospace chapter labels); Blender realism
   only for the one or two objects that carry the story, developing out of the characters and back; one other accent
   at most (the feedback trails) per film. One visual system per film.
10. **Length:** 2:00-3:00 (long enough to count as long-form in 16:9 for watch hours; cut vertical for TikTok/Shorts).
