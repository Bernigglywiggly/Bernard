## Description (paste from here)

On the eighth of October only eight ships crossed the Strait of Hormuz. Before the war about 125 large ships a day went
through. Yet by the end of September, by one tracker's count, Middle East crude exports were back near pre-war levels.
This film explains how: the geography of the 21-nautical-mile gap, how a strait gets "closed" without a wall, the
pipelines and ship-to-ship shuttles that took the oil round it, why the attacks have moved towards those routes this
month, and why oil now has two prices. My verdict: Hormuz works less like a tap than a tollgate. Plus the three things
I'm watching next.

Nothing here is a forecast or financial advice. Every figure on screen carries its source and date.

Maps are drawn for this film: shipping lanes, corridors, pipelines and sea routes are drawn schematically, and attack
positions are approximate. Relief contours are real: NOAA ETOPO 2022 (public domain). Coastlines: Natural Earth.
Narration is a synthetic voice (ElevenLabs). Music: an original drone made in code for this film.
