Pitch

Three to five minutes, in this order. The whole trick is that you talk about their listing, with their numbers, on your phone. Nobody else walking in does that.

1 · The door

10 SEC

Hiya, is the owner about? I'm local, I'll be sixty seconds. I looked you up on Google on the way here and there are two things costing you orders. Can I show you?

"Local", "sixty seconds" and "costing you orders" answer the three silent questions: who are you, how long, why should I care. Asking permission lowers their guard.

Not the owner"When's best to catch them? I'll pop back." Write the time in the notes.
Busy"No worries, what's quiet tomorrow, three?" Book it and leave.

2 · The mirror

40 SEC

This is what people see when they search "chinese near me". You're on 4.1 with 63 reviews. The place on the corner is 4.6 with 400. Google shows three places, and people pick on the stars and how many reviews.

Use their real numbers from the audit, plus one nearby competitor. Hold your phone so they can read it; don't hand it over yet. BrightLocal 2026: 68% of people need at least 4 stars, 47% won't consider fewer than 20 reviews, 74% only look at the last three months.

3 · The cost

40 SEC

How many orders on a normal Friday? … Right. If this brings you two extra orders a week at twenty quid, that's about two grand a year. Harvard looked at this: one extra star was worth five to nine percent more revenue for independent restaurants.

Get them to say their own number and do the sum out loud together. People believe maths they did themselves.

4 · The tap

30 SEC

This is all it is. Tap it with your phone.

Power move: before you walk in, find their Place ID and write their own review link onto a demo card. When they tap it, their own Google review form opens. It's the moment they get it.

5 · The offer

20 SEC

Thirty quid. I set it up now with your link, test it on iPhone and Android, and put it where people pay. If it hasn't brought you new reviews in a month, I'll give you your money back.

Anchor: other shops sell these for £35 to £50 and post them to you to set up yourself. You're on-site, set up, tested and guaranteed.

6 · The close

10 SEC

Do you want it on the counter or by the till?

A choice between two yeses. Take the card payment, fit it, test it with them, take a photo for your portfolio (ask first), and set a follow-up for day 7.

7 · Day 7 and day 30

FOLLOW-UP

You've had six new reviews since Tuesday. Two of them mention the wait on Fridays. Want me to reply to them for you, and sort your hours and photos while I'm at it?

This is where the money is. Results first, then the next rung of the ladder.

Objections

"I haven't got time for this."

"That's why it's a stand. Your customers do it, not you. It takes me three minutes, and I'm gone."

"We're always busy, we don't need reviews."

"Busy's brilliant. Reviews are for the people who aren't regulars yet: someone new in town, students, anyone searching at 10pm. And more reviews means one bad one can't drag you down."

"Too expensive."

"It's thirty quid, one family order. If it doesn't bring reviews in, you get it back. What have you got to lose?"

"We're on Just Eat, that's enough."

"Keep it, it's great for reach. But every order through it costs you up to 30%. Reviews on Google send people to your own phone and your own door, at nothing."

"I need to ask my partner."

"Course. When are they in? I'll leave this with your own review link already on it. Tap it with them tonight and I'll pop back tomorrow at [time]."

"Is this even allowed?"

"Yes, it just opens Google's own review form. We don't give freebies for reviews and we ask every customer, not only the happy ones, which is exactly what Google's rules want."

"Come back later."

"No problem, what's quiet tomorrow? Three o'clock?" Book a time, write it in the notes, and turn up on the dot. Being reliable is half the sale.
