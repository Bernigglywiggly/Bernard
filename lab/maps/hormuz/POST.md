## Description

On 8 October only eight ships crossed the Strait of Hormuz. Before the war about 125 large ships a day went through. Yet
by the end of September, Middle East crude exports were back near pre-war levels. This film explains how: the
geography of the 21-nautical-mile gap, how a strait gets "closed" without a wall, the pipelines and ship-to-ship
shuttles that took the oil round it, why the attacks have moved towards those routes this month, and why oil now has
two prices. My verdict: Hormuz works less like a tap than a tollgate. Plus the three things I'm watching next.

Nothing here is a forecast or financial advice. Maps are drawn for this film; shipping lanes, corridors, pipelines and
sea routes are schematic, and attack positions are approximate.
