## 1. The rules that decide how to contact each firm (UK)

| Firm is a... | Cold marketing email allowed? | Use | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Limited company or LLP ("corporate subscriber") | **Yes**, without prior consent; always say who we are and give an easy opt-out | Email the named person or the general address | ICO business-to-business guidance |
| Sole trader or ordinary partnership ("individual subscriber") | **No**, not without consent or a prior customer relationship | Phone, a letter, or meet in person | ICO |
| Anyone, when the email holds a person's name | UK GDPR still applies: tell them why we have their details, rely on legitimate interests, honour objections | Keep a short note per prospect of where the address came from | ICO; experts disagree on details: follow the live ICO page |

## 4. For D: the draft shape (one email per firm, 90-120 words)

1. Line 1: the firm-specific hook from §3 (a fact about *them*, checked on their site).
2. Line 2: what we'd make, how long, and a link to a 10-15 s sample (the Visa film or a Curve Short; never a client's logo we weren't paid for).
3. Line 3: price range and turnaround ("from £400, ready in a week").
4. Sign-off with his name and business identity, plus "reply STOP and we won't email again" (PECR).

Send order: rows 1-5 first (best fit, highest budgets), then installers (deadline hook), then agents. Venues only once he can visit.
