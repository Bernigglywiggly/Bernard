# The Curve · format bible v2 (28 Sep 2026), from @pollar.news

The user's call: Pollar is the core inspiration. Keep a solid, consistent look and sound, but the topic and the
script are the main thing. No jokes in the lines (the humour lives in the analogies: funny, exact comparisons, said

## What Pollar does (measured 28 Sep 2026: 50 latest videos, the top three transcribed)
- **Topics that win:** AI and big-tech power stories with a hidden mechanism. By views: Nvidia guaranteeing ~$250B so
  OpenAI can lease one campus (4.6M), an OpenAI agent that broke out of its test sandbox (717K), what the Meta glasses
  light really means (550K), then politics and security (300-470K). Plain politics and war updates trail (15-60K).
- **Length:** nearly all 2:30-3:00, a few 1:00-2:00. Vertical 1080×1920, 60 fps.
- **Spoken hooks are concrete and personal, never the headline:**
  "Right now, your phone is drawing about one watt to play this." / "This is the light. Actual size, 2mm wide. It
  sits on a pair of Meta glasses… Maybe of you." / "An AI was given an exam in breaking into computers. It didn't
  just sit the exam. It went looking for the answer key."
- **Shape:** hook (an object, a number, a tiny story) → a scale ladder from the familiar to the huge (1 W phone → 400 W
  home → 1 GW reactor → 7.5 GW Paris → 10 GW campus) → the news, dated and sourced ("This week, the Wall Street Journal
  reported…") → the hidden mechanism ("the light blinks only for saving, not for the camera being on") → what is NOT
  known, said plainly ("Nothing is signed. These are talks, reported by one newspaper.") → a quiet, mirrored close →
  sources on screen.
- **Voice:** calm, declarative, no adjectives, no hype, short fragments for the big beats. 110-150 wpm.
- **Sound:** a low continuous bed; hard 0.1-0.4 s silences right before each reveal.
- **Look:** black, one accent colour, small monospace chapter labels ("THE LIGHT", "THE RULE", "WHO DECIDES"),
  big numbers set in full ("$250 000 000 000"), captions lower-middle. One visual system per film (a grid where area
  equals watts and the camera only pulls back; a network map with a live action log; grey 3D products on a ruler).
  Realism only where an object matters.

## Our rules (The Curve)
1. **Topic:** an AI / tech / power story with a number that can be made physical and a mechanism most people have
   never seen. Current (dated this week or month) beats evergreen; an evergreen story gets a current hook.
2. **Line one is concrete:** an object, a number or a tiny story, often in the present tense, often "you".
3. **Every number made physical:** a ladder from the viewer's hand to the scale of the story.
4. **Sourced and honest:** dates, named sources; say what's reported vs confirmed, and what nobody knows.
5. **Curiosity is the engine:** each line opens a question the next one closes. One clearly labelled IMAGINE
   (not a forecast, a what-if) stretches the viewer's imagination; it is never passed off as fact.
6. **No jokes in the lines, no swearing, no hype words.** Short declarative sentences; fragments for the big beats.
   The humour lives in the analogies (rule 12), never in the phrasing.
7. **Fast:** George at ~1.15-1.2 speed (≈170-190 wpm), tight gaps, but a hard silence before each reveal.
8. **Close quietly:** a mirrored pair of lines, then sources.
9. **Look:** ASCII all the way (characters on black, turquoise accent, monospace chapter labels); Blender realism
   only for the one or two objects that carry the story, developing out of the characters and back; one other accent
   at most per film, and it stays polished: a phosphor trail in the grid (moving things leave a short tail stepping
   down the ramp, nothing else moves), never a zooming, colour-split feedback tunnel. One visual system per film.
10. **ASCII that reads (v3, 28 Sep):** a fine grid (8×12 px characters, 240×90); outlines drawn with stroke glyphs
    (| / - \\) that follow each shape, dense fills (@ # %) for solids, faint glow cells dropped; the subject bright
    white-cyan with a soft light behind it; the drifting background sea faint and cleared around the subject, so
    there is always one focal point; the key number big (at least 12 rows of characters tall); small monospace
    labels drawn crisp on top, never as characters.
11. **Length:** 2:00-3:00 (long enough to count as long-form in 16:9 for watch hours; cut vertical for TikTok/Shorts).
12. **Analogies, as many as it takes (the user, 28 Sep):** every big number gets something people can picture, and
    the out-of-the-ordinary ones are the house style: what a missile *costs* in Big Macs (a Patriot interceptor, about
    $4.2M, is about 680,000 Big Macs; the drone it's fired at, about 5,600), a million dollars a day since the Romans
    invaded Britain, per second, per person on Earth, average UK houses, years of the median salary, notes stacked
    past the space station. Mix the funny with the plain; use as many as the story needs, not one per section. Read
    them straight: the humour is in the comparison, never in the phrasing (no wink, no punchline word). Exact to two
    figures and sourced. The Big Mac ($6.22, The Economist, July 2026) is the house unit for money. Bank and
    calculator: `lab/format/units.py` (`usd`, `gbp`, `item`).
