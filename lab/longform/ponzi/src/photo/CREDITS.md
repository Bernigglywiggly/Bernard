## How the files were obtained

- Wikimedia Commons originals were downloaded from upload.wikimedia.org (full-size original, not a thumbnail).
- Internet Archive pages were downloaded at full scan resolution (scale 1) from the item's own page-image server. Files described as "(crop)" were cut from that page with no other alteration; the full Leslie's page and the three periodical front pages are untouched.
- Digital Commonwealth rights lines were read from the record's own JSON (`rights_ss`, `license_ss`, `reuse_allowed_ssi: no restrictions`); the image file itself was taken from the identical Commons copy.
- loc.gov presented a bot check ("Just a moment..."), which was not bypassed. LOC rights lines are therefore quoted as transcribed on the Commons file pages, which carry the LOC catalogue link. A human should open the two LOC record URLs once to confirm.

## Gaps — nothing safe found

1. **Boston Post front pages, July–August 1920.** The Post is not on the Internet Archive, Chronicling America or Commons; the only scans located are on newspapers.com (paywalled, not an acceptable source). Substitutes kept: two Boston American front pages and the 1921 Editor & Publisher page announcing the Post's Pulitzer.
2. **A 1920 mugshot.** No free 1920 booking photograph was found. Only the 1908 Montreal mugshot exists openly, and only as printed in Leslie's (small).
3. **Ponzi with cane, at his desk, or with his car, as sharp photographic prints.** The good ones are Leslie Jones (rights reserved). Kept alternatives are halftone reproductions from 1920 magazines.
4. **Deportation 1934, Italy, Brazil, the 1949 hospital picture.** Everything found is Leslie Jones (rights reserved) or post-1930 press photography with no clear rights. Nothing kept.
5. **Hanover Trust Company.** Only the one halftone from Leslie's; no clean archival photograph of the building.
6. **Other officials** (Bank Commissioner Joseph C. Allen, US Attorney Daniel J. Gallagher, District Attorney Pelletier, Richard Grozier as a standalone portrait): only thumbnail-size files on Commons. Grozier appears on the kept Editor & Publisher page.
7. **A US-issued or Spanish/Italian reply coupon.** The kept example is the Belgian printing of the same UPU design.

## Notes for a cautious publisher

- **Agency credit lines.** Seven kept pictures carry 1920 credits to Underwood & Underwood, Keystone View Co. or International. US copyright in anything published in 1920 expired on 1 January 1996, so these scans of the 1920 printed page are public domain in the US. Agencies (Getty/Bettmann and others) sell modern scans of the same negatives; that does not revive copyright, but only use the files here, taken from the 1920 printing, never an agency copy.
- **US only.** "Pre-1931 US publication" is a US rule. For the corporate/anonymous 1920–21 press material the position in the UK and EU is also expired (70 years from publication). The two Abdalian street views are the exception: he died in 1967, so life-plus-70 countries could treat them as protected until 2038. BPL states no known restrictions; the practical risk is low but not zero.
- **"No known restrictions" is not a warranty.** LOC and BPL both say it is the user's responsibility; it is the standard basis on which documentaries use Bain, National Photo Company and Abdalian material.
- **Quality.** The magazine and newspaper files are halftone prints scanned from microfilm: fine for full-screen with a slow push-in, grainy if cropped tight. The Bain, National Photo and Abdalian files are from original negatives and are sharp.
- **Courtesy credits** (not legally required for any kept file): "Library of Congress, Bain Collection"; "Library of Congress, National Photo Company Collection"; "Boston Public Library, Leon Abdalian Collection"; "Internet Archive".
- **Captions as printed are not always consistent** (25 vs 27 School Street; the Bain date). Use the dates in this table.
