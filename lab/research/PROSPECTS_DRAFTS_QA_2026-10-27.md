   The reasons:
   - The ICO's B2B rule is "not disguise or conceal your identity".
   - A sole trader who trades under a business name must show his own name and a UK address on business letters (Companies Act 2006 ss.1201–1202). Treat an email as a business letter to be safe.

## Three lessons for future drafts

1. **Check the firm's own video before pitching video.** Look at the site, its YouTube channel and its Instagram, then pitch the gap beside what exists. Five of these 21 offered something the firm already makes. A two-minute check of the channel would have caught all five.
2. **Point to the change, not to their mistake, and quote numbers exactly as the source states them.** No "your page" with a date in the subject, and no "still says" in line 1. Gov.uk BUS: an extra £1,500 (£9,000 total) until March 2027. MTD: the £30,000 threshold is qualifying income for sole traders and landlords, and the £50,000 group started in April 2026.
3. **Identity and proof go in every message, and claims stay true.** That means his full name, a trading name he has actually chosen, a postal address and, once one exists, a link to real work. Do not imply clients he does not have ("I make films for Staffordshire venues") or a location nobody has confirmed ("near Stone").
