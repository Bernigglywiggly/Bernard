## 2. How the tricks work

| System | What it is | What it does | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fans and ground seal ("multi-mode suction") | Fans pull air out from under the car while skirts seal the gap | Up to 2 g of suction, enough to hold it upside down "even when stationary". Corners at over 2 g, against about 1.26 g from the tyres alone (15:09-15:25) | P1, P10 |
| Two aero modes | High downforce and low drag. At low speed the fans suck the car down; at speed the front and rear open up for more flow (15:25-15:44) | Grip at any speed, without needing airspeed | P1 |
| Active rear wing | Variable pitch, adjusted automatically (15:45) | Downforce on demand. The actuators come from Optimus | P1, S5 |
| Cold-gas thrusters | SpaceX-derived nozzles fed by high-pressure gas bottles (§4) | Launch, yaw control, roll in the air, landing cushion | P1, S1 |
| Steer-by-wire | Yoke, brake and throttle all by wire; seats moulded into the chassis (14:11-14:23) | Saves weight; fixed seat position | P1, P10 |

## 4. The SpaceX cold-gas package

| Item | Fact | Tag | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gas | "Ultra high pressure air tank" (Musk, 12:21). Moravy told Top Gear it is filled from a high-pressure **nitrogen** system | P / S | P1, S1 |
| Tanks | COPVs (carbon-overwrapped pressure vessels, based on Falcon hardware) | P / S | P1, S1 |
| Thrust trim | One 200 L COPV in the boot, "up to about 10,000 PSI", feeding **4 rear nozzles** | S | S1 |
| Rocket and the demo car | Two COPVs, **600 L total, about 250 kg of air**, **10 nozzles** (Moravy on stage, 12:11-12:20). Split as 200 L in the rear plus 400 L in place of the passenger seat | P / S | P1, S1, S13 |
| Nozzle layout | 4 at the rear (main thrust) + 4 at the corners pointing down (lift) + 2 at the front (yaw) = 10 | S | S1, S11 |
| Valves | Small electric actuators taken from Optimus; "micro adjustments within a fraction of a second" | S / P | S1, P10 |
| Thrust | The 4 rear nozzles give "over 80 kN" (auto-caption at 18:08 reads "80 kons"). Supersonic exhaust shows Mach diamonds. Moravy: "roughly the same thrust as a fighter jet" | P / S | P1, S1, S12 |
| Uses | Launch (sub-1 s 0-60) · yaw correction · rolls and flips · lift and a soft landing (Rocket only) | P / S | P1, S1 |
| Braking and cornering | Promised by Musk in 2018 ("dramatically improve acceleration, top speed, braking & cornering"); **not demonstrated** in 2026. The fans now do the cornering | P | P12, P1 |
| How long it lasts | Thrust: **one launch per fill**, about 5 s of thrust over the start of a quarter mile | S | S1 |
| Refill | 2-6 h today, target under 30 min (Moravy), against Musk's "the car can recharge its air tank" | S / P | S1, P1 |
| Legal | Rocket: "Not approved for public road use" and off-road only. Thrust is road-legal, but the thrusters aren't for roads (gravel becomes a projectile) | P / S | P10, S1 |

## 5. Physics check [EST]: our numbers, assumptions shown

| Question | Answer | Working |
|---|---|---|
| What does 80 kN do to a 1,700-1,800 kg car? | About **4.5-4.8 g** from the thrusters alone | 80,000 N ÷ (1,700-1,814 kg × 9.81) |
| How long does 250 kg of gas last at 80 kN? | **About 2 seconds** | Cold air or N₂ leaves the nozzle at about 550-750 m/s (theoretical maximum 789 m/s at 300 K), so it uses 107-145 kg/s |
| Why does Tesla say about 5 s for Thrust? | Thrust falls as the bottle empties, so the average is far below 80 kN | 200 L holds about 90 kg at 10,000 psi |
| How long could Rocket hover? | **About 8-11 s at best** | Weight about 17 kN needs 23-31 kg/s; 250 kg divided by that. Pressure decay makes it shorter |
| Is 250 kg in 600 L realistic? | Yes, at about 500-700 bar (7,000-10,000 psi) | 417 kg/m³; matches "up to about 10,000 PSI" |
| Energy to refill 250 kg to about 600 bar | **At least 38-40 kWh** with a perfect compressor; perhaps 60-80 kWh in practice | W = m·R·T·ln(p₂/p₁) = 250 × 297 × 300 × ln 600 ≈ 142 MJ |
| Why 2 g of suction? | Hanging upside down needs suction of at least 1× the car's weight; 2 g gives double margin | — |

**The film line this gives us:** the "flying car" carries about two seconds of full thrust, and
refilling it takes hours.
