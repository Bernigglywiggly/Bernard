## 4. Physics for the diagrams (built now, used in either branch)

### A. How a touchscreen finds your finger (capacitive grid)

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | The screen carries a grid of transparent electrodes in rows and columns; each crossing acts as a tiny capacitor | P1, P3 | V |
| 61 | A finger near a crossing disturbs the electric field and changes that capacitance; the electronics turn the change into X-Y position | P1 | V |
| 62 | One set of lines sends, the other receives; the controller checks every crossing in turn, so it can see several fingers at once | P3, P1 | V |
| 63 | The signal is tiny: a finger is on the order of a picofarad, and on large panels the change can be a few hundred femtofarads | P2 | U (one source) |
| 64 | Apple patented a "multipoint touchscreen" in 2004 (granted 2010): a transparent capacitive layer that reports several touches separately | A5 | V |

### B. Why a laptop screen wobbles when you tap it (torque about the hinge)

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70 | Torque = force x lever arm, the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of the force; it's largest when the push is at 90° | P4 | V |
| 71 | So the same tap twists the screen twice as hard at the top edge as halfway up (twice the lever arm) | P4 + OUR MATHS | OUR MATHS |
| 72 | The reported fix: a stiffer hinge and screen hardware so the display doesn't bounce (row 26) | G7 | REPORTED |

### C. "Gorilla arm": why Jobs said your arm gets tired

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80 | Researchers built a metric ("consumed endurance") for the arm fatigue of interacting with a raised, unsupported arm; it tracks how tired people say they feel | P5 | V (paper exists; findings via extract) |
| 81 | A Purdue-reported HCI researcher (Stuerzlinger): "gorilla arm syndrome" is already an issue with vertical touchscreens | P6 | U (expert view, not a measured laptop result) |
| 82 | Apple's reported answer is to keep touch optional and the trackpad primary (rows 24, 25), not to make you hold your arm up | G5, G4 | REPORTED |

### D. OLED vs today's mini-LED MacBook Pro

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 | OLED pixels make their own light, so each pixel sets its own brightness and needs no backlight (LG Display's words: "self-emissive") | P7 | V (maker's explainer) |
| 91 | Today's MacBook Pro (since 2021) lights an LCD with a mini-LED backlight: up to 1,000 nits sustained full-screen, 1,600 nits peak, 1,000,000:1 contrast | A3 | V |
| 92 | Tandem OLED = two light-emitting layers stacked so their light adds up (Apple's iPad Pro, 2024: 1,000 nits full-screen, 1,600 nits peak HDR) | A2 | V |
| 93 | Makers' claims for laptop tandem OLED: LG says about 3x the brightness and 2x the lifespan of single-layer OLED, up to 40% less power; Samsung's laptop panels reach 1,600 nits peak | P7 | U (makers' claims) |
